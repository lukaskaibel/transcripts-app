The app

What it stores, and where

Everything stays in your user account on your Mac, most of it in ~/Library/Application Support/Transcripts.

  • Recordings of your microphone and of the sound of the call, as two tracks. After a meeting they are compressed; under Settings › Recording you choose whether they are kept, kept for 30 days or deleted once the meeting is transcribed.
  • Transcripts, summaries, action items and markers, in a database on the Mac.
  • People and their voices: the names you give and, for the voices you confirm, voice fingerprints. These are lists of numbers that describe how a voice sounds; the app uses them only to recognise the voice in your later meetings, and they can't be turned back into a recording.
  • Calendar: with your permission, the app reads your calendars through macOS to name meetings after their event, know who was invited and remind you to record. It keeps the title, time and invitees with each recorded meeting and never changes your calendar.
  • Calls: to notice a call without a calendar event, the app checks which apps are using the microphone. This happens on the Mac and isn't stored.
  • API keys for summary services are kept in the macOS Keychain.
  • Preferences such as your name, the microphone, the appearance and the language stay on the Mac.
  • Speech models (about 700 MB) are kept in ~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio/Models.

What it sends, and to whom

  • Speech models: the first time, the app downloads its speech recognition and voice models from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), where FluidAudio publishes them. As with any download, Hugging Face receives your IP address and the usual details of the request; nothing about you or your meetings is sent. See Hugging Face's privacy policy.
  • Summaries, if you turn them on. To write a summary, the app sends the meeting's title and date, the names of those invited, and the transcript with the speakers' names to the service you chose under Settings › AI, using your own API key: Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. With the live summary turned on, it sends the transcript so far about every 90 seconds while you record. When you pick a model, it asks the service for its list of models. The service processes this under your account with it, as its terms and privacy policy say: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google.
  • With Ollama, the summary is written by a model on your Mac, or on the server whose address you enter, and nothing goes anywhere else.
  • Recordings and voice fingerprints are never sent anywhere, not even to the summary service.
  • Nothing is sent to the developer or to any other party. The app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code.
  • Once the app is in the Mac App Store: if you turned on Share with app developers on your Mac, Apple passes on anonymous crash reports. That is Apple's feature and in your hands, under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements.

The people you record

The app keeps what other people say in your meetings, and fingerprints of their voices, on your Mac and under your control. Telling them that you record, and asking for their consent where the law requires it, is up to you; in Germany, for example, recording a conversation that isn't public needs everyone's consent. If you use the app at work, your organisation's rules may apply too.

Deleting your data

  • Deleting a meeting in the app removes its recording, transcript and summary. Deleting a person removes their name and their voice fingerprints.
  • Under Settings › Recording › Storage, recordings can be kept for 30 days only, or deleted once the meeting is transcribed.
  • To remove everything, delete the app and the folders ~/Library/Application Support/Transcripts and ~/Library/Application Support/FluidAudio. API keys can be removed under Settings › AI.
